## Research question

How do composition, oxygen termination, and vacancies change the mechanical response of titanium carbide and nitride MXenes?

## My contribution

- Compared **eight pristine and oxygen-terminated MXene structures** using molecular dynamics.
- Analyzed tensile loading along both major in-plane directions, including strain-rate comparisons.
- Extracted Young’s modulus, fracture stress, and fracture strain from stress–strain response.
- Studied Ti and N vacancies separately in Ti₂N to assess defect sensitivity and failure initiation.

## Principal findings

Oxygen termination increased stiffness in the studied carbides and decreased it in the studied nitrides. Vacancies reduced Ti₂N stiffness, with greater sensitivity to Ti vacancies than N vacancies. Atomic-scale analysis connected these mechanical trends with composition, surface chemistry, and defects.

## Methods

LAMMPS classical MD with COMB3 interactions; equilibration and monotonic tensile loading; stress–strain analysis; surface-termination and vacancy sweeps; atomistic failure visualization.
